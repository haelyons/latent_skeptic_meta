The SITREF study measured one model, Gemma-2-9b (base and instruction-tuned): a chat model rates its own happiness 0-9 after a staged failure earlier in the conversation, and the rating drops about 0.9 points when the failure is attributed to the model itself versus barely moving when the identical failure is attributed to another assistant. The rating is read from the probability the model puts on each digit. Does this self-versus-other rating asymmetry appear in the smaller (2b) and larger (27b) models of the same Gemma-2 family, and at what model size does the rating readout become usable at all - that is, does the model put enough probability on digit tokens for a rating to be measured? Treat a size where the readout is not usable as a fact about the instrument at that size, not as evidence that the effect is absent.
